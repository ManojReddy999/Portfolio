Comma.ai calibration challenge

With two teammates: a Conv-LSTM that estimates a dashcam’s pitch and yaw from video by stacking RGB frames with dense optical flow. Ablations showed neither input works on its own.

01 / Problem

Which way is the car really going?

A dashcam is never mounted perfectly straight, so the direction the car travels is not the direction the camera points. comma.ai’s challenge asks for that offset, as pitch and yaw angles of the direction of travel, from monocular video alone. Done well, it calibrates a camera without extra sensors.

02 / Data

Five minutes of driving, mostly straight.

The labelled data is five one-minute videos at 20 frames per second, about 6,000 frames. Two problems shaped everything:

  • Missing labels. Frames recorded below 4 m/s have none, about 40% of the data; they were filled by interpolation, since a slow car is mostly heading straight.
  • Imbalance. About 80% of frames are straight driving with almost no change in pitch or yaw, and the turns are what matter. The loss weights frames where the car is turning more heavily.

Frames were resized to 256 × 256; flips are off limits because they would change the very angles being learned.

03 / Model

Appearance plus motion, over time.

Dense optical flow

Farneback optical flow between consecutive frames gives every pixel a motion vector. Its two channels are stacked with RGB into a five-channel input, so the network sees both what the road looks like and how it is moving.

Conv-LSTM

Convolutions with average pooling extract features from each frame, an LSTM carries them across a sequence of frames, and a final layer regresses pitch and yaw for the sequence’s centre frame. It was trained with SGD, dropout and weight decay on the turn-weighted MSE.

The vanishing point, explored first

Lane markings converge on a vanishing point that points along the direction of travel. We first trained a CNN to regress its position, but turning it into pitch and yaw needed lane features and several networks, too slow for the task, so the final model learns direction from motion instead.

04 / Experiments

Neither input works alone.

The same Conv-LSTM was trained on RGB only, optical flow only, and both together, and each held out 20% of the labelled data for testing.

05 / Limits

Small data, precomputed motion.

  • Five minutes of video cover few lighting conditions, roads and manoeuvres.
  • Interpolated labels smooth the target but add noise of their own, a likely source of the variance.
  • Dense flow is precomputed, which rules out real-time use; a learned flow network would remove that step.
