Learning to drive from depth alone

Deep RL policies (PPO, SAC) that drive an Ackermann-steered car through cluttered, obstacle-rich environments from onboard depth sensing, built as a graduate student researcher in Prof. Nikolay Atanasov’s lab and deployed on a real F1TENTH car.

01

The task

A 1/10-scale car with Ackermann steering starts in a walled arena scattered with obstacles of different shapes. The policy sees the scene through an onboard depth camera (the image above is a real frame) and outputs steering and throttle. The reward is forward speed plus a small bonus for staying alive, and a collision ends the episode.

There is no map and no goal: the car has to keep moving without crashing, using only what it senses. You can drive the same car in the same arena in the lab, with a classical planner standing in for the policy.

02

What I built

  • The simulator. A custom MuJoCo / dm_control environment: the Ackermann car with a simulated depth camera, randomised obstacle fields, contact-based collision checks, reward and termination.
  • The training setup. PPO and SAC in Stable-Baselines3, with depth, point-cloud and vehicle-state observations.
  • The perception models. Three feature extractors compared on the same task, below.
  • The deployment path. Policies exported from PyTorch to ONNX and TensorRT, running onboard the car’s NVIDIA Jetson TX2.

03

Three ways to see

Each extractor turns what the car senses into features for the same policy head, so the comparison isolates perception.

  • CNN, the baseline: convolutions over the depth image.
  • Graph network: the depth image becomes a point cloud, modelled as a graph so the network reasons about 3D structure rather than pixels.
  • Cross-modal transformer, adapted from LocoTransformer (Yang et al., ICLR 2022), which was built for a quadruped: CNN patch embeddings and MLP-encoded state tokens inside a multi-head transformer encoder, for temporally aware state representations.

04

Results

Each step up in perception made the car faster, let it cover more ground, and crash less.

Graph network vs CNN

Navigation speed+21%
Distance covered+85%
Collisions−17%

Transformer vs graph network

Mean velocity+20%
Average coverage+135%
Collision frequency−27%

The graph network’s 3D view of the scene beat the image-only CNN, and the transformer beat the graph network, which I attribute to its ability to capture spatiotemporal dependencies across the depth frames and the car’s own motion.

05

On the real car

The policies left simulation: I converted them from PyTorch to ONNX and then TensorRT, and ran them onboard the NVIDIA Jetson TX2 of a real F1TENTH car.
