01 / Task

Drive, reach, grasp, carry, release.

The youBot has a four-wheel mecanum base that can move in any direction and a five-joint arm. It has to approach a cube, close its gripper on it, carry it to a goal pose and let go, with base and arm moving together the whole time.

MATLAB computes the robot’s motion with the Modern Robotics library and writes it to a CSV that CoppeliaSim plays back, so every run can be watched.

02 / Controller

Three pieces, one loop.

Kinematics simulator

NextState integrates wheel and joint speeds over each 10 ms step (first-order Euler), with the base’s motion from mecanum odometry and every speed clipped to a limit.

Reference trajectory

Trajectory_Generator chains eight screw-motion segments for the end-effector: to a standoff above the cube, down, grasp, up, across, down, release, back up, with the gripper state attached to each point.

Feedback control

FeedbackControl adds the reference’s own motion (feedforward) to proportional-integral feedback on the pose error, then maps that twist to wheel and joint speeds through the pseudoinverse of the combined base-and-arm Jacobian, with a tolerance that keeps it well behaved near singular arm configurations.

04 / Limits

Kinematics, not dynamics.

  • The simulation is kinematic: commanded speeds are achieved exactly, with no torque limits, latency or contact physics.
  • The integral term has no anti-windup, which is part of why large integral gains overshoot.
  • The next step would close the loop on measured state and validate on hardware under conservative limits.
